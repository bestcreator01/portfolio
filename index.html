Computer graphics — CS5610, 2025

Loading the mesh…

Shading sandbox

Drag to rotate

The Utah teapot, modeled a few buildings over from where I took this class, with the same texture, normal and cube maps from my CS5610 assignments, running live in your browser. Shaded is the bare Blinn-Phong pass, lit from the eye, with the red diffuse and shininess straight out of my fragment shader. Textured adds a diffuse and specular map. Displaced is the tessellation assignment: a flat, heavily subdivided quad with my normal and displacement maps pushing a teapot out of it, which is worth a look under Wireframe. Environment reflects a cube map off the body. Wireframe shows what the shading is actually sitting on.

Genie from the Lamp

Final project · 2025

A gold lamp, a column of smoke, and a genie. The smoke is a real-time particle system emitting from the spout and the ground plane: each particle an instanced cube with procedural motion, turbulence and age-based fading, lit with Henyey-Greenstein and Schlick phase functions for directional scattering and modulated by Perlin-Worley and erosion noise volumes so it reads as cloud rather than fog. The genie rises and fades in above the spout once the smoke has built. All real time, and you can orbit the scene while it runs. The second clip is where this started: smoke trapped in a refractive sphere over a mirrored plane, before I threw out the scene and went looking for a better reason for smoke to exist.

05 · About

How I got here

I am a full-stack engineer with close to three years of production experience, currently building event platforms in Go and React that run live at international events.

I did not start in computer science. I started in film and media arts, because making things is the part I have never been able to put down. In 2020 that meant 3D modeling and concept art, and getting completely absorbed in how digital shapes, lighting and materials are constructed behind the scenes. An intro Python course in fall 2021 turned out to be the most fun I had had in ages, so I switched to computer science the following spring.

The art side never went anywhere. In 2024, several semesters into the degree and already interning, I was still signing up for kinetic arts and embedded systems: a mirror that reads your face, a paper flower wired to an Arduino, a shark that glows in the dark. I like the problems that sit between the two.

These days I work at Xsolla in Los Angeles, where I have been the dedicated engineer on an invite-only B2B matchmaking platform that ran at gamescom asia and G-STAR, and have spent the rest of my time stress-testing our plugin platform the way an outside developer would experience it. Before that I spent two and a half years on enterprise Angular and Spring Boot at the University of Utah.

I use AI tools like Claude Code, Copilot and Windsurf to move faster, but architecture, testing and code review stay in my own hands. I write tests first and think like a tester: ID swaps, tampered requests, the edge case nobody wrote down. Mostly I am drawn to projects that solve a real problem for real people.
